# Changelog

Changes to the GASP Standard and to the `gasp-standard-mcp` package, newest first. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/) and the package follows [semver](https://semver.org/). Changes to the standard's content are listed under the package version that first shipped them, since the package bundles the standard. `DECISIONS.md` has the reasoning behind each rule.

## [Unreleased]

## [1.25.0] - 2026-11-27

### Added
- **A twelfth change in `classify_revenue`, `unpaid_in_term`** (`DECISIONS.md` 2026-11-27): a customer stops paying inside a committed term and the contract stays in force. Nothing in MRR moves, each invoice of the term stays in Billings as unpaid until it is paid or written off and the customer churns on the day the contract ends. The movement table under MRR has a row for it and the calculator a preset.
- A "Three-year contract" preset in the calculator: 36 months at $108,000 counts at $3,000 of MRR, $36,000 of ARR and ACV and $108,000 of Bookings.

### Changed
- **The late payment rule names its day.** A subscription cancelled for non-payment is Churned MRR and a churned customer on the day the cancellation takes effect and the subscription ends, which can fall after the day the payment was missed. The text said "on the day it is cancelled". A contract with a committed term keeps its MRR while the company holds the customer to it. No formula or number changes, and the standard names no number of days past due at which an unpaid contract leaves MRR.
- A late payment's answer in `classify_revenue` lists Billings: the invoice stays there, reported as unpaid until it is paid or written off.
- The calculator asks who the customer is as one choice of four (a new customer, one who holds another subscription, one coming back, one converting from a trial) in place of three boxes. Addresses are unchanged. The preset "One fixed year" is "One year, no renewal".

## [1.24.0] - 2026-11-27

### Added
- **The year-or-renewal test and fixed-term revenue** (`DECISIONS.md` 2026-11-26). A subscription counts in MRR and ARR when its contract runs 12 months or more or when a shorter plan renews, by a renewal provision or by following an earlier term with no break in access. Product access sold for under 12 months with neither is fixed-term revenue, reported on a line of its own. `standard/metrics/core.md` states the test under MRR with two tables, what is sold (22 rows) and what happens when a contract starts or ends (12 rows), and the glossary gains Fixed-Term Revenue: 122 definitions.
- **A `renewable` field** on the `subscription` and `contract_event` datasets: the contract carries a renewal provision or is itself the renewal of an earlier term. `customer_status` gains `fixed_term`, for a customer whose only contract is fixed-term, and `revenue_type` gains `fixed_term`. The ontology has 295 fields.
- **Two readiness checks**, `fixed_term:subscription` and `fixed_term:contract_event`. The first fails on a subscription row with `renewable` false and a term under 12 months, the second on a `new_contract` event of that shape that carries ACV. A mapping without `renewable` leaves both unable to run.
- **Fixed-Term Revenue, a Finance metric**: recognised revenue from product access sold for under 12 months with no renewal, `sum(field.revenue where field.revenue_type = 'fixed_term')`. The standard has 320 metrics, 298 of them computable.
- **A `classify_revenue` tool** (24 tools), which applies the year-or-renewal test to one of three questions. A contract by `kind` (what is sold, its term in months, whether it renews or follows an earlier term, whether the customer holds another subscription, is coming back after churning or is converting from a free trial, its value): whether it counts in MRR and ARR, the line the revenue is reported on, whether its end is churn, what its first and last day are reported as, the amounts and every other metric it reaches (CARR, Bookings, Billings, ACV, NRR, GRR and their adjusted forms, the customer counts, Trial-to-Paid Conversion Rate, Fixed-Term Revenue, Services Revenue, Consumption Revenue, Usage Run-Rate and Prepaid Credit Balance). A contract with up to six `lines`: each line answered by its own kind and term, the totals per metric and a bundle price shared across the lines by standalone price. A `change` to a running subscription (seats added, a new module, a price increase, a downgrade, a price cut, a discount with an end date, a move to annual billing, a one-off credit or refund): the movement it is reported as, its tags on the ledger and the metrics it moves. Each line, movement and metric carries its page and the answer links to the same question in the calculator at https://gaspwiki.com/research/does-it-count, which runs the same functions.
- **Rules for licences, hardware, notice, late payment and stepped prices** (`DECISIONS.md` 2026-11-26), each adopted from the practice of listed companies, the SaaS Metrics Standards Board and the billing vendors. A term licence counts by the year-or-renewal test wherever the software runs. A perpetual licence fee and hardware sold outright are charged once and stay out of MRR and ARR; maintenance on a perpetual licence takes the test on its own term. A right to cancel and notice of a future ending move nothing until the ending takes effect. A subscription with a payment overdue keeps its MRR and is Churned MRR on the day it is cancelled for non-payment. A contract whose price steps up counts each step from the day it takes effect, as Expansion MRR. `core.md` states each under MRR and both tables carry the rows. `classify_revenue` takes three more kinds (`term_licence`, `perpetual_licence`, `hardware`) and two more changes (`notice`, `late_payment`).
- `recurring.ts`, the test as code: `classifyRevenue` answers whether an item counts in MRR and ARR, whether it can churn and the line it is reported on, and `movement` says how a start or an end is reported. The two tables in the standard are its output and a test holds them equal. `assessContract` answers one contract in full, `assessDeal` a contract with several lines and `assessChange` a change to a running subscription, for the tool and the calculator.
- **A fourth consumption basis, `top_up`** (`DECISIONS.md` 2026-11-26): consumption drawn from credits bought once, an automatic top-up included, with no commitment in MRR behind them. The purchase of a pack stays out of MRR, ARR, NRR and GRR. Consumption Revenue counts each dollar as it is drawn and its `by_basis` breakdown gains a `top_up` row. Usage Run-Rate reads `on_demand` and `top_up` consumption, where it read `on_demand` alone, so it is the annualised usage no commitment covers. Prepaid Credit Balance subtracts `committed` and `top_up` consumption, so it carries what is left of a pack. The `credit_balance_identity` check holds a top-up drawdown to the same rule as a committed one. `cel.md` gains example 12, a pack and its automatic top-up. No value over data without the new basis moves.
- **A customer status for pay-as-you-go accounts, `pay_as_you_go`** (`DECISIONS.md` 2026-11-26): an open account with no active subscription that buys usage, on demand or as credits bought once. Total Customers, Logo Churn Rate, NRR and GRR read `active` and leave the account out. Consumption Net Revenue Retention takes `active` and `pay_as_you_go` into its cohort, so an account that let its balance run out counts at zero. No value over data without the new status moves.
- **Each Ossie conformance case as a script for the upstream `ost` runner** (`DECISIONS.md` 2026-11-26): `standard/exports/ossie/conformance/cases/<case>/case.ost` creates the tables the case reads, loads the case's model and asks for the metric through the Apache Ossie view expander. The expanded SQL and the result table under each command are the output of that runner at `apache/ossie` `7fb92a7`. `npm run conformance` holds each table to the values computed on PGlite. The three direct snapshot cases also ask for the readings a filter can state, six queries that show the wrong values. The four readings that change the SQL of the model stay in `readings.sql`. The set grows from 63 files and 1,057 KB to 78 files and 3,732 KB: `ost` has no command that runs a SQL file, so each script carries its tables.

### Changed
- **Breaking: MRR and ARR leave out a contract under 12 months with no renewal** (`DECISIONS.md` 2026-11-26, which supersedes the rule of 2026-11-21 for these contracts). The formulas are unchanged: the contract has no row in the `subscription` dataset and no Revenue_Event, so it is absent from MRR, ARR, CARR, New MRR, Churned MRR, Contraction MRR, NRR and GRR, and its end is neither churn nor contraction. Its `new_contract` event carries the total value and a null `acv`, so it counts in Bookings and stays out of the ACV average. A customer who holds nothing else has the status `fixed_term` and is outside Total Customers and Logo Churn Rate. A second short contract that follows the first is a renewal and counts from its own start. A contract of 12 months or more with no renewal counts as before, until the day it ends. Support, maintenance and managed services sold on subscription terms take the same test; delivered work stays Services Revenue. A company whose subscription data held short contracts with no renewal reports lower MRR and ARR and less churn. A company with none sees the same values.
- **Breaking: Adjusted NRR and Adjusted GRR open on the MRR that is meant to last.** The base is `metric.mrr at period_start` less the temporary increases still running on the first day net of their scheduled reversals, realised usage apart. Before, the base kept temporary revenue while the numerator left its reversal out, so a fixed year with no renewal raised the adjusted rate for the whole of its term. The Ossie cohort table `gasp_account_cohorts` carries the amount as `temporary_opening`. A customer who leaves with a temporary increase running books two `churn` events that day, the reversal tagged `scheduled_reversal` and the rest `permanent`. A company with no temporary revenue in its ledger sees the same values.
- **The MRR and CARR Exclude lists agree with the table under MRR on usage.** MRR leaves out usage billed on demand with no commitment behind it; committed usage counts by the test and usage above a commitment for the period billed. The lists had left all variable usage out.
- The glossary's Active Customer and the description of `subscription_status` say that a subscription with a payment overdue stays active until it is cancelled. The field keeps its four values.
- `change_nature` `temporary` marks a contract or add-on of 12 months or more with no renewal, a discount with an end date and the usage realisation pair. A short add-on with no renewal carries no event, since it never enters MRR.
- **The vendored Apache Ossie validator moves to `7fb92a7`** (2026-11-25, schema `0.3.0.dev0` as before) and the `ost` test runner is vendored beside it. The validator names functions as written and requires `OVER` on a window function (apache/ossie #800) and prints a portability report on request (#799): on the reference model every field and metric is in `OSSIE_SQL_2026`. The schemas and the expression grammar are unchanged.
- **Breaking: Gross Revenue Retention (GRR) and Revenue Churn Rate leave out the reversal of realised usage** (`DECISIONS.md` 2026-11-26). Usage above a commitment enters the revenue ledger as an expansion and a contraction that reverses it the next day. Both rates counted that contraction as revenue lost, so a customer who used more than they committed to lowered GRR. Both now add the reversal back to Contraction MRR, which follows the SaaS Metrics Standards Board's recommendation to exclude variable usage revenue from GRR. A company with no realised usage in its ledger sees the same values. Contraction MRR, Carry MRR, Net New MRR, NRR and Expansion Rate keep their formulas. The cohort table `gasp_account_cohorts` gains a `usage_reversal` column and both rates stay plain sums over it.
- **Breaking: the Market form of Net Revenue Retention (NRR-M) counts every expansion and reactivation of cohort customers, with a tag or without** (`DECISIONS.md` 2026-11-27). The form filtered `expansion_classification` on all six of its values, which left tagged data as it was and dropped an event with no tag. The ledger `derive_cel` writes carries none, so a company on a derived ledger reported NRR-M with no expansion in it. The formula now reads `event_type` alone. Where every event carries a tag the number is unchanged; where events are untagged NRR-M rises by their sum over the opening MRR. NRR-O and ARR-O read the tag as before and the readiness check `expansion_tag:revenue_event` still asks for it.

Opens, clicks, unsubscribes and spam complaints divide by emails delivered, click-to-open by unique opens and bounces by emails sent. Brand Awareness is the share of surveyed prospects who know the brand, with an `unaided` variant, read from the new `brand_awareness` field on `survey_response`. Onboarding Engagement Score averages `onboarding_engagement_score` over the onboardings in progress at period end. Implementation Quality Score averages `implementation_quality_score` over the onboardings completed in the period.

Before, `score_drift`, `compile_model`, `compile_target`, `readiness_report` and the model check passed over a metric a company had declared under a dataset. ### Changed
- **Breaking: five rates follow the population of the first day of the period** (`DECISIONS.md` 2026-11-24). Partner Churn Rate, Cross-sell Rate, Upsell Rate, the `entered_at_risk` variant of At-Risk Rate and Resurrection Rate each divided every event of the period by a count taken on its first day. Each is now a cohort formula: a partner, customer or user counts in the numerator when they were in the opening count. Upsell Rate follows the customers active and eligible for an upgrade on that day and Resurrection Rate the users dormant on it. Partner Churn Rate `by_partner_tier` puts each partner in the tier held on the first day. A published number changes where someone joins and acts inside one period. Upsell Rate also changes where a customer upgrades without the eligible flag. Each rate now stays between 0 and 1; Resurrection Rate could exceed 1 before. Partner Lifetime Value reads Partner Churn Rate and follows. Upsell Rate now needs `customer_status` on the customer dataset.
- **A cohort condition on the status field states the membership** (`DECISIONS.md` 2026-11-24). A cohort is the keys its dataset counts as active on the cohort's day and a `where` narrows it. A condition that reads the field of the dataset's active rule replaces the rule: `over cohort(field.user_id, period_start where field.user_status = 'dormant')` is the users dormant that day, where it compiled to an empty set before. No formula used that shape until Resurrection Rate.
- **A snapshot dataset states its reading rule and a dataset's description is its label** (`DECISIONS.md` 2026-11-24). Each of the 25 snapshot datasets in the Ossie files carries `ai_context.instructions` with its grain and the rule to select one snapshot date; the 68 metrics of the reference model that repeated the rule no longer do. The "Kind:" and "CEL class:" sentences leave the dataset descriptions, since the GASP extension on the dataset holds both. `compile_model` keeps the rule on each metric, because it leaves a company's datasets as written. No metric name, expression or expected value changes.
- **The Ossie files declare a metric under the dataset it reads** (`DECISIONS.md` 2026-11-23). A metric that reads one dataset is written in `datasets[].metrics` with single-part field names (`SUM(mrr)`). A metric that reads several datasets stays in the model-root `metrics` with `dataset.field` names. In `gasp.semantic_model.yaml` 349 of the 384 metrics move and 35 stay at the root; in the Core example 29 of the 39 move; the 15 conformance case models have no root `metrics` list. `compile_model` writes a company's model the same way. Metric names, extension payloads, staged sources and every expected value are unchanged. A consumer that reads the root `metrics` list alone must read `datasets[].metrics` too. The Relational Query Interface upstream exposes dataset-scoped metrics only, so the three direct conformance cases now run through the upstream ost view expander as published.
- The formula id `email_metrics` is removed with its entry. `lookup_metric` resolves each of the six email metrics by name. "Email Metrics" is now the name of their section and resolves to nothing, so `search` for "email" is the way to list them. On the site `/v1/metrics/marketing/email-metrics` answers 301 to the Email Open Rate page.
- The vendored Apache Ossie validator moves to upstream `4a315fa` (2026-11-22, nine commits on; #780, #782, #792 and #793 touch the vendored files). The script rejects an aggregate function with no `OVER` clause in a field's `OSSIE_SQL_2026` expression, declares `sqlglot>=30.29.0` and warns on a sqlglot too old to check SQL. Every published file passes with the same output and no exported file changes.

## [1.22.0] - 2026-11-21

### Added
- The tools read two forms Apache Ossie has proposed and not yet merged (`DECISIONS.md` 2026-11-21). Vendor and dialect names are read in any case (apache/ossie #712): `gasp`, `Gasp` and `GASP` name the same extension and `snowflake` and `SNOWFLAKE` the same dialect. A dataset `source` written as a table object, `{kind: table, identifier: [...]}` with each part a name or `{name, level}` (apache/ossie #704), is read as its parts joined by a dot. A document in either form gives the same `validate_mapping` report and the same compiled model as the current form, and a tool that hands the document back writes the form the pinned validator accepts: `GASP` and the dialects GASP reads in uppercase, the source as a string. A table part that needs quoting (a dot, a space, a leading digit) and a source of any other `kind` are refused with the dataset named, since the proposal forbids a consumer to flatten one or read it as another kind. Another vendor's name and a dialect GASP does not read keep their spelling.
- Two variants of CARR that take churn off before it takes effect (`DECISIONS.md` 2026-11-21). `net_of_known_churn` is CARR less twelve times the temporary MRR still running and less the ACV of each cancellation made by period end for a contract that ends later. `net_of_forecast_churn` is CARR less the shortfall of `forecast_renewal_amount` against twelve times MRR, for each active subscription with a forecast whose term ends after period end. They are alternatives and are never added together. `get_formula` prints both and the Ossie export stages each as its own metric, `gasp_carr_net_of_known_churn` and `gasp_carr_net_of_forecast_churn`.
- The event model states what a `cancellation` on the contract ledger carries: the day the contract ends in `contract_end` and the annual recurring value that ends in `acv`. It also states how a temporary change that ends early or is kept is booked. The synthetic dataset's three cancellation rows follow the rule, which changes no metric value.
- **CARR (Contracted Annual Recurring Revenue)**, also called Committed ARR, as a Finance metric and the graph node `carr` (`DECISIONS.md` 2026-11-21): ARR plus the Annual Contract Value of each new contract signed on or before period end whose start date is later, `metric.arr + sum(field.acv where field.contract_event_type = 'new_contract' and field.contract_start > period_end) to_date`. It follows the SaaS Metrics Standards Board's definition, under which churn and downgrades come off on the day they take effect. ARR is unchanged. 314 metrics, 302 formula entries, 105 graph nodes and 213 edges.
- The Ossie export stages Net Revenue Retention (NRR) and Gross Revenue Retention (GRR) through one cohort table, `gasp_account_cohorts`, with one row per customer per reporting period over the customers active on the period's first day. Its columns are opening MRR and the period's movements for that customer, each declared as a GASP formula and compiled per customer. Nine entries read it as plain sums (NRR with its O, M and adjusted entries, GRR with its per-customer and adjusted entries and the `by_segment` breakdown of each), so a consumer can slice retention by any attribute joined on the customer or list the customers behind a change. The table carries the segment each customer held on the period's first day as a column, which the two breakdowns group by. The other seven give the values their per-period datasets gave. `coverage.json` marks the nine with `cohortTable: true` and still counts them as staged with reason `cohort`. ARR-O, Commit Accuracy and Consumption Net Revenue Retention keep their per-period datasets. `DECISIONS.md` 2026-11-20 and 2026-11-21. Revenue Churn Rate and Expansion Rate joined the table on 2026-11-21 with their `by_segment` breakdowns, 13 entries in all (see Changed).
- `compile_model` writes the same table over a company's datasets and field names, in its warehouse dialect, with only the columns the selected metrics sum. `compile_target` writes it for each engine: a view in the Snowflake staged script with its amount columns as facts, a Cube cube keyed by period and customer whose measures sum its own columns and a dbt model with a surrogate key over period and customer and a sum measure per column. The customer's segment is a dimension in each.
- The Ossie portability profile reads the full upstream ladder, M0 to M8 (it stopped at M3, the last rung published when it was written). `coverage.json` tags each direct metric with the highest rung whose construct it uses: M4 a metric across two datasets, M5 arithmetic over aggregates (metric composition with the expressions inlined), M6 a date or time scalar, M7 and M8 window functions. Of the 193 direct entries 20 are on M2, 43 on M3, 113 on M5 and 17 on M6. M4 holds none, since each GASP aggregate reads one dataset and a cross-dataset entry therefore combines two aggregates; M7 and M8 hold none, since a formula that needs a window is staged. The `constructs` list and its counts are unchanged.
- The Ossie conformance set grows from 11 cases to 15 and now grades a result. Four new cases cover which rows of a snapshot dataset a reporting period reads: June MRR, second-quarter MRR and Total Customers for June as direct expressions read on the last snapshot date, and Logo Churn Rate over four weeks that begin on the day a customer starts. A case may list readings, each a value the generator computes on the fixture: the value a named mistake returns (MRR summed over June's 30 dates gives 695,900 against 24,200; three month ends added give 64,800; customers counted over every date give 19 distinct or 525 rows against 18) or the value another reading in use returns (the opening count taken at the prior close gives 1/13 against 1/14). `manifest.json` carries the grading rule, right, alternative, wrong or failed, and per case its `kind`, `group`, `period` and `readings`; a case with readings has a `readings.sql`. Win Rate and the six-month Logo Churn Rate case gain one wrong reading each.
- `gasp-ossie-validator`, the checker behind `validate.py --vendor GASP`, is on PyPI (0.1.0, https://pypi.org/project/gasp-ossie-validator/): its own licence file, author and classifiers, a README that names the install from the index (`uv run --with gasp-ossie-validator validation/validate.py --vendor GASP <file>` from a clone of apache/ossie) and a release workflow in the public repository that builds it, runs the built wheel over the published GASP files and uploads it through trusted publishing.

### Changed
- Contraction MRR is worded as every reduction in committed recurring revenue from customers who remain customers, with or without a change in service (`DECISIONS.md` 2026-11-21). Its list adds price reductions, discounts and contract changes that lower the monthly value, with a worked move from monthly to annual billing at a discount. The formula and every published value are unchanged.
- The Industry equivalents line under ARR no longer lists CARR and CMRR as other names for ARR: Committed ARR and Baseline ARR stay, in the consumption sense of commitments without uncommitted usage, and CARR points to its own metric. A lookup of "CARR" resolves to the new metric.
- MRR, ARR and the Change Nature section of the CEL state how a fixed-term subscription or a temporary add-on is counted (`DECISIONS.md` 2026-11-21): in MRR and ARR until its contract ends, as contraction or churn on that day, with `change_nature` (`temporary`, then `scheduled_reversal`) as the filter. A whole subscription sold for a fixed term takes the same pair as an add-on. Text only: no formula and no value changes.
- **Revenue Churn Rate and Expansion Rate follow the customers active on the first day of the period.** A governed change to two published formulas (`DECISIONS.md` 2026-11-21), taken because the vendors and bodies that publish a definition count this way: `(metric.churned_mrr + metric.contraction_mrr) / metric.mrr at period_start over cohort(field.customer_id, period_start)` and `metric.expansion_mrr / metric.mrr at period_start over cohort(field.customer_id, period_start)`. A movement counts when the customer was active on that day, so Revenue Churn Rate equals 1 less GRR over the same period and stays between 0 and 1. Before, the MRR lost or gained by a customer who joined inside the period was in the numerator and absent from the denominator. Both `by_segment` breakdowns count each customer in the segment held on the first day. The value moves only for a company with a customer who joins inside a period and loses or gains MRR in it and for one with a customer who changes segment in it. LTV and the LTV:CAC ratio read Revenue Churn Rate and move with it. Both metrics now need the `customer` dataset. In the Ossie export the two entries and their breakdowns read the cohort table (13 entries, 158 staged datasets where there were 162) and ten entries are staged for the cohort (26, was 16) where they were staged for the opening of the period (20, was 30). The example demo's expected values for Revenue Churn Rate, LTV and LTV:CAC change.
- **Logo Churn Rate follows the customers active on the first day of the period.** A governed change to a published formula (`DECISIONS.md` 2026-11-21): `metric.churned_customers / metric.total_customers at period_start over cohort(field.customer_id, period_start)`. A churned customer counts when they were a customer on that day, so the rate stays between 0 and 1. Before, a customer who joined and churned inside the period was in the numerator and absent from the denominator. Logo Retention Rate follows through `1 - metric.logo_churn_rate`. Both `by_segment` breakdowns count each customer in the segment held on the first day and read `segment` from the customer snapshot alone. The value moves only for a company with a customer who joins and churns inside one period or changes segment and churns in it. In the Ossie export the four entries are staged for the cohort (16 entries, was 12) where they were staged for the opening of the period (30, was 34). The conformance cases `c03` and `c15` keep their expected values and readings, with the cohort's membership filter added to their queries.
- **NRR and GRR by segment read the customer's segment at period start.** A governed change to two published breakdowns (`DECISIONS.md` 2026-11-21). Each customer active on the first day of the period counts in the segment the customer snapshot gives them on that day, with every snapshot and event of theirs in the period. Before, each row counted in the segment it carried, so a customer who changed segment during the period had opening MRR in one segment and movements in another. The value moves between segments and only for a company with such a customer: the company-wide NRR and GRR are the same. The two breakdowns now need `segment` mapped on the `customer` dataset and no longer read it from `subscription` or `revenue_event`. The rule is the compiler's: under `over cohort`, a breakdown by a field the cohort key's dataset carries groups the members.

- The CEL derivation rules: `standard/cel.md` gains "Deriving Revenue Events from a Subscription Ledger", the vendor-neutral rules that turn the GASP `subscription` dataset (snapshots or a version history, at any cadence) into `Revenue_Event` rows: a step is a change in a subscription's active MRR, the ledger's first date is the opening balance, one event per step dated the snapshot that first shows it, `prior_mrr` and `new_mrr` are the customer's MRR chained through the customer's steps so the row identity holds, the event type from the customer's MRR around the step, ATL lifecycle tags and defaults, an empty `expansion_classification` because the ledger cannot say what kind of expansion it was and the netting a coarse cadence causes. `src/cel.ts` writes the SQL with the compiler's placeholders in all four dialects (`revenueEventDerivation`, which `fillTemplate` compiles over any mapping that covers the six fields), and `test/cel.test.ts` runs it over the synthetic dataset: every generated movement comes back row for row, the monthly sums agree with the compiled MRR movement metrics, month-end snapshots give the same net change dated the month end and the SQL printed in `cel.md` is the one the module writes. - `record_decision`: the Decide step (`src/ossie/decide.ts`), closing readiness level 2. It records the decisions people make on a profiled and proposed warehouse. Each decision names what it is about, its subject by content and its owner: a profiler question answered (the key, the time field, the kind or a datatype, written into the model), a candidate accepted or rejected (a table as a GASP dataset, with its columns matched as the proposer would; a column as a GASP field, with an optional transform that becomes a calculated field), a metric owner (the slot `validate_mapping` reads) or a judgement call GASP cannot apply (which system owns customer identity, the reporting currency, what date marks churn), recorded with its answer. Every decision lands in the mapping's GASP extension; a later decision on the same subject replaces the earlier one. With no decisions it lists what is open: the questions no decision or model state has settled, the candidates no decision has settled and the covered metrics without an owner. Level 2 is reached when no question and no candidate is open. The profiler now writes its questions in full into each dataset's GASP extension and the proposer its candidates and unmatched tables into the mapping's, so the step reads the documents alone. - `propose_mapping`: the mapping proposer (`src/ossie/propose.ts`), readiness level 2. It takes the semantic model `profile_schema` returned (or a company's own, or a schema profiled on the way in) and proposes a GASP mapping: every table is scored against every GASP dataset on how many of its columns find a field there, its profiled kind, key and time field against the dataset's declared ones and its name; every column of a proposed table against the dataset's fields by the same name, the structural role (the table's time field is the dataset's date, its key the dataset's key), a short synonym and abbreviation list, the same words in another order or a near name, each checked against the warehouse type and, for an enum field, the listed values. A column in cents gets a `{{value}} / 100` transform and enum values the standard does not use a `CASE` recode when the recode table knows them (upgrade to expansion). Only high-confidence pairs enter the mapping, an Ossie mapping over the model with a calculated field per transform; medium ones are listed as candidates with their evidence and what would settle them; the rest are unmatched. What the names cannot settle (a span table's date, two foreign keys and no own id, a per-period table with no id, an opaque type) is listed as a question for the Decide step and left out of the model. On the synthetic dataset's own schema the names alone give every table the ontology's time field and kind but one and the key of all but five; statistics settle three more and leave one honest tie. Fixture: `test/fixture/acme-schema.sql`.
- `score_drift`: drift scoring by result (`src/ossie/drift.ts`).
